# Cinematic Adaptations: Aragorn

Adapting beloved characters from a book to a movie is a critical part of the media adaptation process. Every reader has a different idea of how a character looks, walks, and sounds. So it's inherently impossible to satisfy all readers with a cinematic version. I'm going to humbly offer my thoughts on what the Peter Jackson films did well and where they missed the mark when adapting Aragorn, Heir of Isildur.

## Good character adaptions

A good character adaptions needs to succeed in the following areas:
#### 90% Accurate Physical Casting
Tolkien describes Aragorn's first appearance to frodo as tall(6'6"), with dark, long shaggy hair, flecked with grey. There's a more obscure description in the appendices that say he was somewhat grim to look at unless he smiled. But he seemed worthy of honor when he wasn't hiding his true shape. Tolkien uses a lot of soft descriptions which gives a casting director somehow both a lot of wiggle room and also very little. You need someone who can be dressed up to look rugged and unsightly/grim while also having the appearance of also being worth of honor and respect.

#### Dialogue and performance 
Both writers and the actor have to nail this or the character will fail entirely. Aragorn's lines of dialogue can fit into a few different categories: Leadership/Resolve, Wisdom/Encouragement, and Kingly Authority.

Leadership/Resolve - Spoken 
> "Not if we hold true to each other. We will not abandon Merry and Pippin to torment and death. Not while we have strength left. Leave all that can be spared behind. We travel light. Let’s hunt some Orc."

Wisdom/Encouragement - Spoken genuinely and profoundly in a gentle tone
> "Deeds will not be less valiant because they are unpraised."

> "In sorrow we must go, but not in despair. Behold! we are not bound for ever to the circles of the world, and beyond them is more than memory."

> "His grief he will not forget; but it will not darken his heart, it will teach him wisdom."

Kingly Authority - Spoken with nobility. No doubt or trepidation could be found.

> "Behold the Sun setting in a great fire! ... But behold! The Sun is also a herald of the day that is to come."

> "You forget to whom you speak,"

While dialogue from the original source can be paraphrased and added to, if the writers miss the mark on what Aragorn speaks to different characters and in each story beat, then it isn't Aragorn. If Viggo Mortensen delivers the lines in a way that is not in line with how Aragorn would speak, then it isn't Aragorn. Stars have to align.

#### Character Arch
While this is deeply intertwined with dialogue, you can have 90% of your dialogue and delivery nailed, but if the character arch is off, you end up with something fans of the original material will despise. Aragorn is Tokien's embodiment of worthy kingship. He doesn't grow from flawed to heroic. He's a leader forged by decades of quiet preparation and hardened by war with responsibility greater than himself. During the story of the Lord of the Rings, Aragorn transformed from quiet protector, to visible sovereign. He earns his namesake through service, rather than using his birthright as a tool. 

## Did Peter Jackson get Aragorn right?

#### What did the movies get right?

Viggo Mortensen nails the shaggy but noble look from my subjective standpoint. While he's not nearly the height described by Tolkien, height in the LotR movies is so covered up by movie magic it's irrelevant and is far from the most important physical attributes for the character. He's got the speckled grey in his beard and the costuming was on point.

For Aragorn, the dialogue and delivery felt textbook. Many of the original quotes were used verbatim like, ______. Some quotes that other characters say were used by Aragorn like ______ and some quotes that Aragorn said in the books were used by other characters like _____. This is very common in Movie Adaptations and is okay, as long as the characters are not greatly altered.

#### Rejection of responsibility 

While Aragorn starts out as a quiet protector and ends up as the worthy king in the movies, the why and how is the biggest flaw in this adaptation. Tolkien's Aragorn is always aware of who he is and his responsibility. He's ashamed of his blood, but he's preparing and waits for when he needs to take the throne. In the movies, he is initially portrayed as only ashamed and has fully accepted that Mankind has failed. 

By having Aragorn reject the responsibility of his own blood, his overall purpose is corrupted from rightful king demonstrating his worthiness. Instead, he's a man who is ashamed of his lineage and someone who, despite knowing on good authority that he is the rightful heir, rejects the responsibility he has been assigned.

That said, this attitude appears to be abandoned by Boromir's death(spoiler). I see this as PJ's attempt to fit Aragorn into the reluctant hero trope. The problem is Tolkien's Aragorn wasn't the meant to fall into that model. In an oversimplification, Tolkien wrote Aragorn's journey as more mythic rather than phycological.

## Capturing the Tolkien's intent

While Aragorn ends up in essentially the same place as the books, PJ modified Aragorn's transformation to be a typical hero story rather than a mythic legend. However, so much of Aragorn's behavior and attitude was accurately represented. When I watch LotR, I see Tolkien's Aragorn. But when I see Aragorn speaking to Arwyn about how he doesn't want to be king because he's a man, and men fail, I get frustrated. All it would take to fix this character, would be a few changed lines. Since the miss step does very little to alter the story as a whole and doesn't effect the actor's performance or the majority of the dialogue.
